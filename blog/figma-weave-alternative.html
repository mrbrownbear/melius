The best Figma Weave alternative for generative design

Alex Chen, Member of Technical Staff
Alex Chen, Member of Technical Staff
What Weave does wellThe frictionThe Melius approachWho should switchFAQ

Figma Weave, the product that started as Weavy before Figma acquired it, is a strong idea: a node-based generation canvas with real editing tools, compositing, masking, color grading, and a Timeline for sequencing, plus a set of one-click workflows surfaced right inside Figma Design. For designers who already live in Figma, having generation that close to their work is compelling.

That tight coupling is also the reason people search for a Figma Weave alternative. If you don't live in Figma, or you want a standalone workspace with an agent and more predictable costs, the ecosystem fit becomes a constraint rather than a feature. Here's how Melius compares.

What Figma Weave does well

Weave combines generation with genuine editing, compositing, masking and grading, not just prompt-in, image-out, and its node graph makes each step visible and re-runnable. The June 2026 Timeline brought video sequencing and sound onto the same canvas, and the embedded workflows make common design tasks a click away for anyone already in Figma. As a design-adjacent generation layer, it's well built.

Where teams hit friction

  • No agent, fully manual. This is the biggest gap. Where the rest of the field has some kind of AI agent, Weave's node graphs are wired and run by hand, so someone has to build every pipeline. There's also no announced Slack, MCP, API or CLI to drive it from elsewhere.
  • Credits burn fast. A dominant complaint: users report thousands of credits consumed for a few minutes of work, with iterative fixes for AI mistakes, effectively paying to troubleshoot the tool, eating still more.
  • Ecosystem lock-in. Weave's value is tightly coupled to Figma's world, and there's no audio or voice. If your team isn't Figma-first, much of the advantage doesn't apply.
  • Mid-acquisition uncertainty. Some embedded tooling and the full Figma-to-Weave sync are still rolling out through beta, so the roadmap is moving while Figma folds it in.

How Melius approaches the same work

Melius is a standalone, agent-first workspace, so the thing Weave leaves to you, building and running the graph, is exactly what the agent does.

  • An agent instead of manual wiring. Mel reads your brief, picks a model per step, wires the node graph, runs it, and writes each per-model prompt, so multi-step work starts from intent, not hand assembly. It also runs in Slack and can carry your brand context and skills into every generation.
  • Drivable from your own tools. A canvas-level MCP means an external agent like Claude can build and run the Melius canvas from your own environment, rather than binding your generations to a single design suite. Several agents can also work one canvas at once with visible presence.
  • Generation, audio and assembly in one place. Image, video and audio models (including voice and music) live on the same canvas, with an in-app multi-track editor to sequence and export, all under a single subscription with up to 8 seats on self-serve plans, unlimited seats on Enterprise, and unlimited agent usage.
  • Real-time multiplayer for the team. A whole team works the same canvas live, without depending on one design ecosystem's rollout schedule.
  • Assets you can actually find. Every generation and upload lands in a semantic asset library you search by what's in the image, not a flat pile of files to scroll through later.

Who should switch

If your team is Figma-first and mostly wants the deepest pixel editing next to your design files, Weave's editing and integration are a real advantage. If you want an agent that builds and runs the pipeline for you rather than wiring every node by hand, drivable from your own AI tools, with audio and one subscription across every model, Melius is the more independent, agent-first home for that work.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Figma Weave alternative?

Melius is the strongest standalone alternative to Figma Weave. Both are node-based generation canvases with broad model access, but Melius is an independent creative workspace rather than a layer tied to the Figma ecosystem, it adds Mel to plan and wire multi-step work, and it keeps every model under one credit pool.

How is Melius different from Figma Weave?

The biggest difference is the agent. Figma Weave has no AI agent, so its node graphs are wired and run by hand, and it is deeply tied to Figma's ecosystem. Melius is a standalone, agent-first workspace: Mel builds and runs the graph from a brief and also works in Slack, its MCP is canvas-level so an external agent like Claude can drive the canvas from any host, and every model shares one subscription.

Is Figma Weave the same as Weavy?

Yes. Weavy was the original node-based generation canvas; Figma acquired it and rebranded it Figma Weave, folding it into Figma Design. So a Weavy alternative and a Figma Weave alternative are the same search. If you want that node-canvas-plus-generation model but without the Figma coupling, Melius is a standalone equivalent with an agent and one shared credit pool.

Why do people look for a Figma Weave alternative?

The dominant complaint is credit cost: users report credits burning extremely fast, sometimes thousands of credits for a few minutes of work, with iterative fixes for AI mistakes eating more. There are also reports of billing friction on cancellation, and some tooling is still in open beta. Teams outside the Figma ecosystem also want a standalone option.

Does Melius integrate with design tools?

Melius exposes an MCP server, so an agent like Claude can read and act on your canvas from your own environment. Rather than binding generation to one design suite, it aims to be model-agnostic and host-agnostic so it fits whatever workflow you already use.

Can Melius sequence video like Weave's Timeline?

Yes. Melius includes an in-app multi-track editor for arranging shots, timing and layering audio, alongside the node canvas for building the generations that feed it.
