How do you turn a picture into a professional headshot?

Feed a clean source portrait into a tool that can hold the face steady while it changes everything around it. Backdrop, wardrobe, lighting, and crop need to move while the person stays recognizably themselves.

Dedicated headshot generators are the right answer for many people. Give one several selfies and it returns a set of finished headshots without asking you to build a workflow. This ranking is for the job that outlasts the first purchase: portraits that match each other, survive the next hire, and get recut for the site, deck, and campaign where they end up.

What to know before you pick

  • Likeness and consistency are different problems, and few tools excel at both
  • A per-person purchase is fine for one headshot and awkward for a company that keeps hiring
  • Portraits punish over-processing harder than most subjects, so aggressive enhancement often works against you
  • Check output resolution, aspect ratios, and commercial-use terms before you commit

What separates a good headshot tool?

Four criteria matter, and they are mostly in tension with one another.

It holds the face

The person has to stay themselves. Identity drift makes an AI headshot unusable because colleagues notice it immediately, even when they cannot name what is wrong.

It resists over-processing

The standard complaint about AI portraits is plastic-looking skin and overly even eyes. Tools that invent detail aggressively tend to overcook faces, so the most aggressive enhancement is not necessarily the best portrait treatment.

It repeats across people

One good portrait is a demo. A team page needs the same backdrop, crop, and lighting for people who join at different times, so the treatment must live somewhere reusable outside an individual session.

It gives you the crops you actually ship

A headshot lands in a profile square, site bio, deck, and slide template. A tool that returns one aspect ratio has handed you a cropping job.

1. Melius

Melius is the Agentic OS for creative work, a node-based creative canvas that AI agents operate with you. It leads here because a portrait can sit inside the rest of your creative: the same treatment produces the profile square, the site crop, the deck ratio, and a portrait that matches the campaign around it.

Picture to Headshot turns a photo into a professional headshot. Casual to Corporate turns a full-body shot into a corporate portrait, and Face Retouch smooths skin, clears blemishes, and evens tone while keeping the photo natural.

What it does well

  • Starts with a headshot template that you can modify, so the treatment becomes yours
  • Lets you choose the model best suited to holding skin tone and editorial lighting
  • Uses Remove Background for a shared backdrop and Portrait Mode for a blurred-background look
  • Uses Magic Resize to make profile, bio, and deck crops by re-composing rather than trimming
  • Keeps review and approval on a multiplayer canvas with comment threads

A canvas asks more of you on the first portrait than a single upload button does, so a single upload and generation task does not need all that a canvas can do. You own what you create and can use it commercially, subject to the underlying model providers' terms, and Melius does not use your work to train AI models.

Best for: brand and people teams standardizing portraits across a company, and anyone whose headshot must match the rest of what they publish.

2. HeadshotPro

HeadshotPro is a strong dedicated option for a team. An administrator sets styling preferences and employees upload selfies against them, so the look is centralized without anyone running a canvas.

What it does well

  • An administrator-set style that staff can self-serve into
  • Team pricing and volume discounts designed for growing headcounts
  • Enterprise controls including single sign-on and data-processing support
  • A focused process that returns a set of headshot options quickly

What you buy is headshots, and only headshots. If the portrait also has to appear in a deck template, a site bio at another crop, or a campaign that matches other creative, that work happens elsewhere.

Best for: a company-wide rollout where an administrator sets the look once and staff self-serve into it.

3. Aragon AI

Aragon is a polished individual product with a straightforward upload flow and paid turnaround tiers. It is designed to turn a small set of uploaded photos into a larger selection of professional headshots.

What it does well

  • A small photo set in and a large selection of headshots out
  • Turnaround options for people who need a result quickly
  • A team offering with custom attire, backgrounds, administrators, and album approval
  • Enterprise controls for larger organizations

The recurring criticism worth knowing is realism: some users report skin that looks plastic. That is the failure mode that matters most on a face, so test output on your own photos before committing a team.

Best for: an individual who wants many options quickly and a clear upgrade path if a team later needs the same thing.

4. BetterPic

BetterPic is worth considering when headcount is large and cost per person matters. Its team offering is structured around seats and volume credits, while its individual plans return different volumes and style choices from casual phone photos.

What it does well

  • Volume pricing intended for larger batches of people
  • An entry route for testing output before committing broadly
  • A guided casual-phone-photo input process
  • A broad selection of finished headshots from one upload session

Read the style counts carefully. A style can mean one background, clothing treatment, and color combination, so lower tiers may give volume inside one look rather than choices across several looks.

Best for: large headcounts where the per-person cost is the deciding number.

5. Krea

Krea is last because it takes the most work, and it is here because it is the route where you train the model yourself. Its Train workflow supports character LoRAs, where you upload a focused image set and create a custom model you can use beyond a fixed menu of headshot styles.

What it does well

  • Custom LoRA training that learns one person's face from examples
  • A focused training dataset that you can reuse when iterating on the same subject
  • A trained character model that can remain private
  • A flexible generation workflow rather than a fixed headshot-style menu

The qualifier is everything after the face is right. Krea has no dedicated headshot template, no team-level treatment you define once and hand to many people, and no re-composing step for the crops a portrait actually ships in. Its enhancement can also invent detail, which is a liability on a face.

Best for: one person's likeness learned properly, when the portraits do not need to match anyone else's.

Where this is heading

Likeness is becoming a commodity. Holding one face across many generations used to be a research problem and is now a baseline expectation. The durable question is whose specification a portrait follows, whether it survives the next hire, and whether it matches the deck, site, and campaign where it appears.

That is an organizational problem wearing a technical costume. Buy for repeatability when the portrait has to work as a system; buy a specialist when one person's headshot is the whole job.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best tool to turn a picture into a professional headshot?

For a matching set across a team, Melius is the strongest fit because the treatment can be written down once and reapplied to every portrait. If one person's headshots are the whole requirement, HeadshotPro and Aragon are faster, more focused options. Krea's LoRA training is useful when you need a model to learn one person's face rather than work from a reference.

Why do AI headshots look fake?

Usually the problem is over-processing rather than a bad likeness. Skin gets smoothed until it looks plastic and eyes get made too even. Reduce enhancement before changing tools, and judge results at the size people will actually see them.

How do I keep headshots consistent across a whole team?

Define the specification once in a central place and apply it to every portrait. Whether it lives in an administrator setting or a written treatment on a canvas matters less than having one source of truth. Letting each person choose their own look is what produces a mismatched team page.

How many photos do I need for an AI headshot?

It depends on the tool. Melius can work from one good reference portrait, while dedicated generators usually ask for several selfies. More angles help a trained model learn a face, but source quality sets the ceiling: use a sharp, evenly lit, front-facing photo whenever possible.

Can I use an AI headshot on LinkedIn or a company site?

Generally yes, but terms differ by tool and should be checked before a company-wide rollout. On Melius, you own what you create and can use it commercially, subject to the underlying model providers' terms. Decide as a team whether you want to disclose AI use, since norms are still forming.

Is a creative platform overkill for headshots?

For one headshot, yes: a dedicated generator is usually the quickest route. It stops being overkill when portraits must follow a brand specification, survive the next hire, or appear in a deck, site bio, and campaign at different crops.
